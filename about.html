About

Narek Ohanyan

Climate & Environmental Researcher | Expert | Educator | Activist

Narek Ohanyan is an interdisciplinary environmental researcher and global climate youth leader with over 10 years of dedicated experience in climate & environmental resilience and literacy, biodiversity conservation, and environmental policy. Bridging the gap between computational ecological modeling and climate policy, he translates complex planetary data into actionable, on-the-ground strategies.

Currently serving as a Climate & Environmental Researcher at the AUA Acopian Center for the Environment and as a Guest Scientist alongside the Swiss Federal Institute for Forest, Snow and Landscape Research (WSL), Narek builds validated machine learning pipelines to forecast climate-driven structural vulnerabilities. Beyond the data, he is a formidable voice in international policy. Having served as the UN Youth & Children High-Level Climate Champion and the Executive Lead Organizer for LCOY Armenia, he ensures that localized ecosystem adaptation and youth leadership are permanently institutionalized on the global diplomatic stage.

He is the author of the independent research monograph The Overshoot: Life After the 1.5°C Limit, and a certified trainer who has architected over 50 projects and delivered hundreds of capacity-building workshops to democratize environmental intelligence.

The narrative timeline

A decade, descending

  1. The Catalyst

    My entry into environmental science was an immediate response to ecological vulnerability. At age eleven, I confronted the advancing threat of desertification in my home region of Ararat and joined my first local eco-club. This early exposure to localized climate threats laid the permanent groundwork for a career dedicated to sustainability.

  2. Expanding the Foundation

    Theoretical understanding quickly transitioned into active stewardship. I expanded my involvement to the Armenia Tree Project (ATP) eco-club and other regional environmental initiatives, focusing on biodiversity conservation and community-level ecosystem defense.

  3. First Community Project

    Leadership requires execution. By the age of fifteen, I transitioned from a participant to an organizer, launching my first independent community project, “Strong Community — Strong Country”. This marked the beginning of my operational project management experience, focusing on actionable, on-the-ground environmental problem-solving.

  4. UNICEF Green Campaign

    Local actions scale through global frameworks. I initiated my first major institutional collaboration by partnering with UNICEF for a national Green Campaign. This engagement bridged the gap between grassroots youth mobilization and international organizational standards.

    Read the UNICEF story ↗

  5. Founding Climapolis

    To address the critical lack of localized environmental education, I co-founded the Climapolis startup project within the framework of the GenU project of UNICEF in Armenia. By authoring an interactive educational board game, I pioneered the use of gamification to teach complex climate science and adaptation strategies to young demographics.

  6. Global Diplomacy at COP27

    My advocacy scaled to the highest levels of international climate diplomacy. I was appointed as the UN Youth & Children High-Level Climate Champion for Central Asia and Europe, and represented Armenia’s youth voice at the UNFCCC COP27 in Egypt, negotiating directly with global decision-makers.

  7. Academic Integration at AUA

    Recognizing that effective policy requires robust data, I pursued a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science with a minor in Environmental Studies at the American University of Armenia (AUA). This interdisciplinary focus allowed me to integrate advanced computational logic, algorithms, and GIS with critical climate science.

  8. U.S. State Department SUSI Exchange

    Selected as a U.S. Government alumnus, I completed the elite Study of the U.S. Institutes (SUSI) program on Environmental Issues at Shippensburg University. This intensive academic residency solidified my expertise in international sustainability governance, cross-border environmental policy, and strategic leadership.

  9. Entering the AUA Acopian Center

    I formalized my research career by joining the AUA Acopian Center for the Environment as a Junior Expert. Here, I began synthesizing regional environmental data, co-architecting bilingual citizen science toolkits, and translating complex mitigation data into actionable youth curricula. Today I am already a researcher, leading teams and projects.

  10. Educator & Certified Trainer

    Effective climate adaptation requires widespread environmental literacy. As a Certified Trainer in Non-Formal Education by the European Schoolnet and local NGOs, I architected and delivered over 100 interactive capacity-building workshops. Utilizing advanced pedagogical frameworks and the SHUTTLE methodology, I trained educators, NGOs, and youth workers across the region in climate & environmental sciences, environmental conflict transformation, sustainable waste management, and Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR).

  11. Executive Leadership at LCOY

    Operating at the intersection of logistics and policy, I served as the Lead Executive Organizer for the Local Conference of Youth (LCOY) Armenia 2025. I managed comprehensive national agendas, mobilized hundreds of young stakeholders, and synthesized Armenia's official youth policy demands for the UNFCCC.

  12. UNICEF Inclusion Consultant

    True climate resilience requires radical inclusivity. I joined UNICEF as a Consultant focusing on Children and Youth Inclusion. I worked to map organizations of persons with disabilities and advocated for integrated social protection policies, ensuring marginalized voices are fundamentally included in climate strategies.

  13. Publishing The Overshoot

    I published my independent research monograph, The Overshoot, synthesizing climate reality and the systemic implications of crossing thermal thresholds. The work critiques traditional mitigation frameworks and establishes a rigorous, data-driven resilience model for navigating a post-1.5°C world.

  14. Climate Researcher & Project Lead

    Advancing my role at the AUA Acopian Center for the Environment, I now operate as a full-time Climate & Environmental Researcher. I actively design and direct the full lifecycle of complex environmental interventions, bridging computational ecosystem analysis with the strategic execution of community resilience programs.

  15. Directing LCOY for the Second Year

    Entrusted with the national youth climate mandate for a second consecutive year, I reprised my role as Executive Lead Organizer for LCOY Armenia 2026. I continued to refine the institutional architecture required to elevate evidence-based youth adaptation demands to the global stage.

  16. Guest Scientist at WSL, Switzerland

    In direct partnership with the Swiss Federal Institute for Forest, Snow and Landscape Research (WSL), I work on a validated machine learning pipeline to forecast climate-driven forest dieback. Manipulating multi-terabyte datasets and satellite imagery, this pioneering research models a multivariate Forest Vulnerability Score to map structural resilience out to the year 2100.

  17. The Face of COP17

    As Armenia prepares to host the UN Convention on Biological Diversity (COP17), my decade-long trajectory in environmental science and diplomacy has culminated in being featured as one of the primary faces of the COP17 public awareness campaign. Across billboards, urban infrastructure, and cinematic features, my mission remains unchanged: demanding rigorous, data-backed ecosystem restoration and adaptation.
