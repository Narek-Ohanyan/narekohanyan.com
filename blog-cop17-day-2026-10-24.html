Day 6 in Yerevan: A Saturday Without Plenary, With Cities and Scientists on Stage and Hard Choices Waiting for Sunday

24 October 2026 · 5 min read

COP17 Biodiversity Children & youth Policy

Illustration: Narek Ohanyan, using the COP17 butterfly symbol.

Saturday was a different kind of day at COP17. According to the plenary's own schedule, there were no formal negotiating meetings, so delegates caught their breath while cities, scientists and cultural groups filled the programme. Behind the calm, a long list of disputed text is waiting for Sunday.

The day in negotiations

Friday evening's stocktaking plenary told delegates what to expect. According to the UN Transcripts record of that meeting (an automatic, unofficial transcript, so numbers and names need care), there would be no formal meetings on Saturday, Sunday would be a working day with contact groups, and the next plenary was planned for the evening of Wednesday 28 October. I found no report of a plenary or working group on Saturday, so this recap does not describe one.

What matters is what is left open. The Earth Negotiations Bulletin (ENB) summary of Friday and the working group transcripts show that many texts are approved with brackets, meaning wording that countries still dispute. Working Group II reported that talks on biodiversity and climate change will meet again on Sunday morning. The transcript also lists Sunday contact groups on the science body IPBES, sustainable wildlife management, protected areas and synthetic biology.

The working group chairs also gave a sense of pace. Working Group I said its contact groups had met 18 times, and that digital sequence information and the financial mechanism were moving slowly. Working Group II said two Cartagena Protocol items and nine main COP17 items were still under consideration. The budget group, which is working on the Secretariat's 2027-2028 budget, only began negotiating on Friday.

There is also a deadline. The President asked countries to hand in their credentials, the papers that confirm who may speak and vote for them, by Monday 26 October. According to the Bureau's report, 136 of 175 registered parties had submitted credentials for COP17 so far.

Positions and stakeholders

With no plenary statements to report on Saturday, the clearest picture of positions comes from Friday's records, which set up the fights ahead:

  • Soil and subsidies. Russia and others wanted harmful-subsidy language deleted or softened, while the EU, Switzerland, Norway, South Africa and Bhutan defended keeping it (UN Transcripts, Working Group II). The chair sent the question to a Friends of the Chair group.
  • Money for the Nagoya Protocol. Switzerland and Norway questioned a dedicated funding window, while South Africa wanted it kept; a small group led by Switzerland was asked to find wording (UN Transcripts, Working Group I).
  • Benefit sharing under Article 10. India, Japan and the EU wanted references deleted; South Africa and Uganda wanted them kept. Those paragraphs stay bracketed until the relevant contact group reports (ENB).
  • Indigenous peoples and local communities. ENB reports that delegates are still debating how the new subsidiary body under Article 8(j) will work, and set up an informal group on how its Indigenous co-chair would be chosen.

The public programme carried on. The 9th Summit for Subnational Governments and Cities took place on Saturday. Armenpress, citing Armenia's Ministry of Environment, reported that COP17 President Hambardzum Matevosyan, who is also Armenia's environment minister, opened it by saying cities and communities are key to turning global commitments into results, and that they need finance, technology and expert support. He also spoke of new options for communities to set up local protected areas in Armenia and announced an initiative to make the Hrazdan Gorge Yerevan's first community-protected natural area. Other Armenpress reports earlier in the month described a Yerevan city council decision about the gorge, so the exact legal status is something to check.

IUCN, the global conservation union, says cities and regions hold many of the powers needed for land-use planning, protected areas and restoration, and that much local action is missing from national reports. The UN Transcripts site also lists the Science Forum, the Nature and Culture Summit and a capacity-building forum for 24 October, but their transcripts were empty when I looked, so what was said is not reported here.

Numbers to know

  • 136 of 175: parties registered for COP17 that had submitted credentials by Friday evening, according to the Bureau report in the UN Transcripts record.
  • 18: times Working Group I's contact groups had met by Friday (UN Transcripts).
  • 23: agenda items assigned to Working Group II, with nine COP17 items and two Cartagena items still open (UN Transcripts).
  • 26 October and 28 October: the credentials deadline and the next planned plenary.

What to watch tomorrow

  • Whether Sunday's contact groups on climate, wildlife, protected areas and synthetic biology remove brackets.
  • Whether the Friends of the Chair group settles soil, subsidies and pollution wording.
  • Whether the Article 8(j) informal group agrees how the Indigenous co-chair is chosen.
  • Whether countries meet Monday's credentials deadline.
